Who we are

YNAN is a non-profit organization dedicated to empowering primary school, high school and undergraduate students to pursue careers in neuroscience. It serves as a dynamic platform for collaboration, mentorship, professional networking, award celebrations, education and training, advocacy and public awareness. Beyond fostering academic and research excellence, YNAN is committed to advancing innovative and translational neuroscience research, ensuring that young scholars in Nigeria have access to the opportunities they need to thrive and remain competitive in global scientific advancements.

About us

Why YNAN
was founded

Our founder, Dr. Chinna Orish (MBBS, PhD), had a passion for neuroscience but for a long time no access to mentorship, until her mentor, Professor Amadi Ihunwo of South Africa, introduced her to the field. During postdoctoral training in the United States in 2016, she was struck by how well-equipped undergraduate students there were to conduct molecular neuroscience research, an opportunity that was scarce in Nigeria. In 2018, at a high school outreach under the TReND for Africa initiative, a young student asked her: “Ma, can you train us?”

That question revealed a significant gap in neuroscience engagement for young people in Nigeria: under-representation, lack of mentorship and limited exposure to scientific careers. It became the seed that grew into YNAN, formally established in 2019 so that no passionate mind is left behind in the world of neuroscience.

“The lack of mentorship in neuroscience gave birth to this unique initiative.” — Chinna Orish, MBBS, PhD

Our Objectives

What we set out to do

Objectives

  • Encourage participation in research and paper presentations at local and international conferences
  • Encourage students to publish review articles in high-impact journals
  • Encourage student participation in Brain Bee competitions
  • Develop local and international collaboration between young scientists, neuro-practitioners and international organizations
  • Encourage excellence through ECO awards for outstanding students
  • Encourage youth participation in Brain Awareness Week programs
  • Teach primary and high school students about neuroscience through outreaches, seminars and other educative activities
  • Run a journal club
  • Create awareness of neurological conditions and approaches to dealing with them
  • Let students cultivate their interest in neuroscience in an informal environment, without the pressure of being graded
  • Encourage high professional achievement through lectures, seminars, outdoor presentations and workshops
  • Encourage the creation and operation of NeuroSchool

Our Values

  • Respect: kindness and compassion for everyone, whatever their background, culture or opinions
  • Integrity: honesty, transparency and accountability
  • Professionalism: in every event, meeting and communication
  • Inclusivity: diversity, equity and inclusion in all we do
  • Responsibility: ownership of our actions and decisions
